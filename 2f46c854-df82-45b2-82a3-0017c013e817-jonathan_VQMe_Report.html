Section 1 – The Disconnect Between Your Strengths and Your Experience

The Pattern

Seeking Fulfillment (10-), Enjoying Whatever You Are Doing (17-), and Making a Difference (17-) suggest you're questioning whether this leadership role is giving you what you need — or whether you're making the impact you want to make.

This isn't about your capability as a leader. It's about how you're interpreting your leadership experience while you're in it.

A Real-Time Example

You're in a team meeting where performance issues need to be addressed. The trigger is clear: your team isn't hitting targets, communication is breaking down, and you can see the impact on morale.

Your thought: "This isn't what I signed up for. I'm putting in all this effort, but nothing's really changing. Maybe I'm not cut out for this."

Your emotion: Frustration mixed with doubt.

Your action: You address the issues, but part of you is checked out. You might be more direct than usual or find yourself avoiding the deeper conversations that could actually help.

Your result: The team responds to your energy. Issues get surface-level attention, but the underlying problems persist. You feel more convinced that either you or the role isn't working.

Thought → Emotion → Action → Result

The meeting itself — the performance issues, the team dynamics — that's the external trigger. What you control starts with how you interpret that trigger. When you think "this isn't working," everything above that thought shifts: your emotional state, your leadership presence, and ultimately the team's response. The pattern reinforces itself because the results feel like evidence that your initial thought was correct.

What Changes When You Shift Your Thinking

When you shift toward your Having Courage & Conviction and Balancing Compassion & Accountability, you stop measuring your leadership effectiveness by how fulfilling it feels in the moment. You start measuring it by the value you're creating — even when that value isn't immediately visible or personally satisfying.

You become someone who can sit in team meetings fully present, knowing that your role isn't to feel fulfilled by every difficult conversation, but to create the conditions where your team can do their best work. You stop waiting for leadership to feel different and start leading from your strengths regardless of how it feels.

Your days begin to feel more aligned — not because the challenges disappear, but because you're no longer questioning your capacity to handle them while you're handling them.
