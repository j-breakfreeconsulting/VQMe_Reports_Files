Jonathan, what overwhelm is really measuring

A VQMe Internal Dissonance Report for Jonathan

Jonathan,

Time challenges feel urgent because they are urgent. When you're juggling priorities, struggling with delegation, and watching work-life boundaries dissolve, it makes sense that finding more hours feels like the answer.

But what if the pressure you're feeling isn't just about time? What if it's about the way you're measuring whether you're doing enough?

Section 1 – The Weight of Never Quite Measuring Up

There's a particular kind of exhaustion that comes from feeling like you're always falling short of your potential. You work hard, you meet your responsibilities, but there's this persistent sense that you should be doing more, being more, contributing more. The gap between where you are and where you think you should be becomes its own burden.

The Pattern

Your VQ Profile shows a pattern worth examining. When you're looking at your work through the lens of Seeking Fulfillment (10-), everything becomes a reminder of what's missing rather than what's being accomplished. Seeing Value in All You Do (24-) means you're likely viewing much of your work as necessary but not necessarily meaningful — a means to an end rather than something with inherent worth.

This creates a specific kind of overwhelm. It's not just about having too much to do. It's about doing a lot of things that don't feel like they count toward the life you want to be living.

A Real-Time Example

The trigger might be as simple as looking at your calendar for the week ahead. You see meetings, tasks, obligations — all necessary, none optional.

Your thought: "I'm just getting through this. This isn't the life I wanted to build."

The emotion that follows is a mix of frustration and resignation. You feel trapped by your own success, committed to things that don't energize you.

Your action becomes mechanical. You show up, you execute, you check boxes. But you're not fully present because part of you is always elsewhere — thinking about what you'd rather be doing or wondering if this is all there is.

The result is that you accomplish everything on your list but feel empty at the end of the day. Which reinforces the original thought that you're not living up to your potential.

Thought → Emotion → Action → Result

The trigger is external — your calendar, your obligations, the demands on your time. But what you control begins with how you interpret those circumstances. When you shift the thought from "I'm just getting through this" to "This is part of building something meaningful," everything above it shifts too. Your energy changes, your presence changes, your results change. Trying to force different actions or emotions without changing the underlying thought won't hold.

What Changes When You Shift Your Thinking

You stop measuring your days by what's missing and start measuring them by what you're building. When you're anchored in your Having Courage & Conviction and Balancing Compassion & Accountability, you become someone who sees the thread connecting your current work to your larger purpose.

Your calendar doesn't change, but your relationship to it does. You're no longer someone enduring your responsibilities — you're someone who chose them because they serve something you value. Delegation becomes easier because you're not trying to control every outcome. You're trusting the process and the people around you.

Work-life boundaries become clearer because you're not trying to escape from work — you're integrating it into a life that makes sense to you. Overwhelm starts to dissolve because you're no longer carrying the weight of feeling like you're in the wrong place.
