A Real-Time Example

Think about the last time someone suggested a way to help with your workload — maybe offering to take something off your plate or recommending a different approach to priorities.

  • Trigger: Someone offers input on managing your workload
  • Thought: They don't get it. Their way will just create more complications and expectations.
  • Emotion: Resistance, maybe frustration
  • Action: You politely decline or find reasons why their approach won't work
  • Result: You stay overwhelmed, they stop offering help, and the pattern reinforces itself

Thought → Emotion → Action → Result

The trigger — someone's offer to help — is outside your control. But your interpretation of that trigger is where your power lies. When you shift the thought from This creates complications to This person sees value in what I'm doing and wants to contribute, everything above it shifts. The resistance eases, the emotion changes, and suddenly delegation becomes collaboration rather than loss of control.

What Changes When You Shift Your Thinking

The overwhelm doesn't disappear because you found better time management. It disappears because you stopped thinking that accepting help diminishes your authority.
