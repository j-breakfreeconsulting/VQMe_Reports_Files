A Real-Time Example

Think about the last time someone offered you career advice or suggested a different approach to something you were working on. The trigger is external — their suggestion, their feedback, their attempt to guide you.

Your thought: "They don't understand my situation. This feels like they're trying to control or limit me."

Emotion: Resistance, frustration, maybe even resentment.

Action: You dismiss their input, maintain your current approach, or give polite agreement without real engagement.

Result: You stay isolated from the very insights and connections that could accelerate your advancement.

Thought → Emotion → Action → Result

The trigger — someone's advice or guidance — is outside your control. But your interpretation of that trigger is completely within your control. When you automatically frame input as control or limitation, you cut yourself off from the collaborative relationships that leadership requires. The thought is the foundation. Change how you interpret others' input, and your emotional response, your actions, and ultimately your results all shift with it.

You stop measuring your current role against some imagined future version of yourself and start finding ways to create value exactly where you are. You stop waiting for advancement to feel fulfilled and start using your natural courage and conviction to make a difference right now. People begin to see you as someone who can handle both feedback and responsibility — exactly what organizations look for in leaders.

Instead of staying relevant through learning new competencies, you become relevant through the way you engage with what's already in front of you.
