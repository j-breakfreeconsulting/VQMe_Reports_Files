When advancement feels disconnected from who you are

While you're thinking about advancement and leadership development, several patterns are creating friction underneath. A Real-Time Example

You're considering a leadership development opportunity or thinking about what competencies to build next. That's the trigger.

Your mind goes to: "This is what I should be doing to advance, but does this actually matter? Am I just checking boxes? Everyone else seems to find meaning in this stuff, but I'm not sure I do."

You feel disconnected, maybe slightly frustrated or resigned.

You either go through the motions of professional development or avoid it altogether, telling yourself you'll figure it out later.

The result: You stay stuck between knowing you want to grow and feeling like the available paths don't align with what actually fulfills you.

Thought → Emotion → Action → Result

The trigger — thinking about career advancement — is real and external. What you control begins with how you interpret that trigger. When you think "this doesn't really matter" or "I'm just going through the motions," everything above that thought shifts: your energy drops, your engagement decreases, and your actions become half-hearted. But when you start with a different thought — one grounded in your actual values and the difference you want to make — your emotional state, actions, and results transform with it.

What Changes When You Shift Your Thinking

You stop measuring career growth by external markers that don't resonate with you. Instead, you become someone who evaluates advancement opportunities through the lens of your Courage & Conviction — asking whether this path aligns with who you're becoming, not just what you're supposed to achieve.

You start seeing leadership development as a way to amplify the difference you want to make rather than as something you should do to get ahead. Your days begin to feel more intentional because you're building competencies that serve your actual values, not just your resume.

You approach staying relevant from a place of curiosity about how you can contribute rather than fear about being left behind. Professional growth becomes connected to who you are instead of separate from it.
