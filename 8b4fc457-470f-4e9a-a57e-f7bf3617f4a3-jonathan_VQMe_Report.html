That’s a quieter, more honest signal than most people give themselves permission to name. The real challenge isn’t how to get to the gym—it’s how to reconnect with the part of you that wants to want something at all. When “having passion” becomes the goal, it means the drive itself feels out of reach, crowded out by obligations, routines, or the sense that life is something to be managed rather than lived.

The overlooked comment—“want to have passion”—isn’t about laziness or lack of discipline. It’s about feeling cut off from your own reasons for caring. When motivation feels like something you have to wait for, it’s easy to get stuck in a loop: “I’ll start when I feel it,” but the feeling never comes, because the pattern keeps you waiting.

You don’t have to force motivation—you just have to notice what feels true for you, and let that guide the next small move. Sometimes, the spark comes after the action, not before.
