A Real-Time Example

Let's say you're considering starting a new fitness routine or addressing your stress management.

  • Trigger: You notice your energy is low again, or you realize you haven't prioritized your health this week.
  • Thought: "I should be doing more, but I don't have time for myself right now, and besides, most advice doesn't really apply to my situation anyway."

  • Action: Either you push yourself into an unsustainable "all-or-nothing" approach, or you postpone making changes because it feels overwhelming.
  • Result: The cycle continues — either burnout from overdoing it or continued dissatisfaction from not addressing what you know needs attention.

Trigger → Thought → Emotion → Action → Result

When you shift the underlying thought from "I should be doing more but can't" to something grounded in your worth and capacity, everything above that shifts too.
