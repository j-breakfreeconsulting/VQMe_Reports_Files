Nathan, what if advancement isn't about adding more competencies?

Nathan,

I read through your VQMe report and your note about wanting to advance, develop as a leader, and stay relevant through learning new competencies. That focus on acquisition — more skills, more knowledge, more credentials — makes complete sense in today's rapidly changing landscape. And there's something deeper here worth exploring.

When Growth Feels Like You're Swimming Upstream

Looking at your cognitive profile, there's a tension that might be making career advancement feel harder than it needs to be. You have extraordinary Courage & Conviction and Balancing Compassion & Accountability — these are your natural leadership strengths. You can stand firm in your values while remaining genuinely open to different perspectives, and you know how to hold people accountable without sacrificing compassion.

But several patterns suggest you might be approaching advancement from a place of depletion rather than strength.

The Pattern

Your Seeking Fulfillment (10-) and Enjoying Whatever You Are Doing (17-) reveal something important: work feels like a means to an end rather than something that energizes you. You're likely thinking, "I need to do this to get there" instead of "This work itself has value." When combined with Seeing Value in All You Do (24-), there's a underlying current of resignation — like you're playing a hand you were dealt rather than choosing your path.

This shows up as pushing through tasks, accumulating competencies, and checking advancement boxes while feeling somewhat disconnected from the work itself. Making a Difference (17-) suggests you might question whether what you're doing actually matters, which makes it harder to show up with the energy and presence that natural leadership requires.

Your Openness to Advice and Opinions (10-) and Respecting Systems & Authority (17-) create additional friction. You resist being told how to advance or develop, preferring to figure it out your own way. This independence can be a strength, but it might also be cutting you off from mentorship, feedback, and opportunities that come through traditional channels.

A Real-Time Example

Think about the last time you were told about a development opportunity — a training program, a stretch assignment, or feedback about leadership skills you should build. Your initial thought might be something like, "They don't understand my situation. I know what I need better than they do." You feel a familiar resistance, maybe some frustration about having to jump through hoops or prove yourself in ways that feel arbitrary. You might go through the motions, but without real enthusiasm. The result? You complete the requirement but don't gain much from it, reinforcing your belief that these development paths aren't really for you.

Thought → Emotion → Action → Result

The external circumstance — a development opportunity — triggers the thought that others don't really understand what you need to succeed. This creates frustration and resistance, leading you to either avoid the opportunity or engage minimally. The result reinforces your pattern of going it alone, which can limit the very advancement you're seeking. The key insight: you can't control what opportunities are offered, but you control how you interpret them. When you shift from "They don't get it" to "What value might this hold, even if it's not obvious?", everything above that thought — your emotional response, your engagement level, the ultimate result — shifts with it.

What Changes When You Shift Your Thinking

When you start leveraging your Courage & Conviction not just to resist external direction but to explore what might serve you, advancement becomes less about accumulating credentials and more about stepping into your leadership capacity. You stop measuring your progress by how many competencies you've checked off and start measuring it by how much value you're creating and how aligned you feel with your work.

You become someone who can hear feedback without feeling controlled. You can engage with traditional development paths while maintaining your independence. Your natural ability to balance compassion and accountability becomes more visible because you're not operating from a defensive crouch.

Your days begin to feel less like endurance tests and more like opportunities to use your strengths. You stop waiting for the perfect role or the right circumstances to feel fulfilled and start finding ways to create meaning in your current situation while building toward what's next.
